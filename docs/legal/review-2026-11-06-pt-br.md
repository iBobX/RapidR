# Legal review addendum, 2026-11-06: Studio and RapidR in Brazilian Portuguese (the 120 `# legal` texts)

Lawyer-agent review of the pt-BR translation of the texts that carry legal weight (`ide/lang/pt-BR.po`: 98, `crates/rapidr-value/lang/pt-BR.po`: 22), as listed in `docs/legal/i18n-pt-legal-texts.md`. A careful risk review, **not legal advice**. Nothing was edited or committed in the `.po` files; this file only lists what to change.

- **Ids.** `S1`-`S98` are the numbers of the Studio list in `i18n-pt-legal-texts.md`; `V1`-`V22` the numbers of "RapidR's own text".
- **Compared against:** the English msgids already approved (`review-2026-10-29-mcp.md`, `review-2026-10-29-app-eyes.md`, `local-ai-review.md`, `six-traps-preview4.md`) and the Spanish review `review-2026-10-30-es.md` (its R1, R2, R4, R5 findings are checked again here, because the same traps are in the Portuguese).
- **Read:** all 120 texts; a script confirmed that the document is the same text as the 120 `# legal` entries in the two `pt-BR.po` files (120 of 120 equal, English and Portuguese, both plural forms). Code read for context: `ide/settings.inc` (`AIStoreName`), `ide/ai.inc` (key notes), `crates/rapidr-ai/src/keys.rs`, `rai.rs`, `docs/ai-tools.md` and `crates/rapidr-value/src/environ.rs` (what the sandbox does).
- **Test applied to each text:** (1) is every condition of the English still there (what is sent, to whom, what it costs, what stays on this computer, "never", "may cost money", "under 18", "private data", "ask a parent or teacher"); (2) is any sentence a stronger promise or a weaker warning than the English; (3) can a Brazilian reader take away a less protective meaning; (4) plain pt-BR with você, no Portugal words, and the terms of `docs/i18n/pt-glossary.md`.

## Overall verdict: OK WITH CHANGES

The translation is faithful. No consent is broadened or narrowed, no warning is softened, no condition is dropped: "may cost money" is in S48, S59; "private data" in S61, S63, V14, V16; "under 18 / ask a parent or teacher" in S24-S27, S36, S61; "in China" in S26; "Off by default" in S40, S74; "nothing is sent until you press Send / send it yourself" in S30, S34, S74, S78; every "never" sits where the English has "never" (S4, S53, S60, S73). The Portuguese is plain, with você throughout and no Portugal words (no ecrã, ficheiro, utilizador, palavra-passe, premir, definições). Terms follow the glossary (chave de API, senha, chaveiro, provedor, app de IA, nuvem, Bloqueio escolar, Tutor, Excluir / Limpar / Remover).

But **6 groups of changes (27 texts) are needed before pt-BR ships**: 2 places where the "only on this computer" or "who may see" condition can be misread (the same two traps as the Spanish R1 and R5), 1 age wording that has a different meaning in Brazil, 2 sandbox sentences that are unclear or over-promise, the "Allow everything" lead that reads oddly, and the key-location sentences, which are ungrammatical in Portuguese (contractions) and, in RapidR's own text, still show English words. Plus 7 recommended changes. No change to the meaning of any consent is needed beyond these.

## The translator's doubts

1. **"centavo de dólar" for US cents: keep.** In Brazil "centavo" means the centavo of the real, so "um décimo de centavo" would be read as reais and understate the cost several times over (the providers bill in US dollars). "De dólar" is the right fix and it is the same fix the Spanish review required (R2). It is in all four cost texts (S11, S24, S25, S26) and "US$" in the settings caption and V5. No "¢", no "cent". Do not add IOF, exchange rate or card fees: RapidR does not charge, the provider shows the real bill, and a tax figure in the text would go stale.
2. **"responsável ou docente" for "parent or teacher": keep "responsável"; "docente" is acceptable.** "Responsável" is the word Brazilian law and schools use for the adult in charge of a child: "pais ou responsável" (ECA), "pelo menos um dos pais ou pelo responsável legal" (LGPD art. 14, paragraph 1). It covers a guardian who is not a parent. The English asks that this adult *make the key* (their own account and contract with the provider); it does not ask them to consent for the child, so nothing more is needed, and a teacher is fine for that. "Docente" is correct but formal; a 12-year-old says "professor". It is not a legal difference. If the glossary owner prefers the plainer word, "professor" (generic, as Brazilians use it) works in the six child-facing lines (S24-S27, S36, S61) and "docente" can stay in the teacher-facing School Lock lines (S73 and the Bloqueio escolar dialogs). Optional.
3. **"Bloqueio escolar" for "School Lock": keep.** "Bloqueio" is the usual Brazilian word for a lock the owner of a device sets (bloqueio de tela, bloqueio parental), "escolar" says whose it is, and the .po uses it the same way in 21 entries. The name is not in the 120 texts themselves (only in the translator's note), so no change here. "Tutor" for "Coach" is also fine; in Brazil "tutor" has a second, legal meaning (guardian by "tutela") that does not clash with "responsável" in these texts.
4. **Age ("13", "18").** Kept as in English; 13 is GitHub's own rule, not Brazil's, and 18 is the age of majority in Brazil. No change. See the Brazilian-law notes.
5. **"sob os termos de privacidade dele".** Faithful: it points to the provider's terms and names no law. Leave it that way; see the recommended "política de privacidade" wording.
6. **School Lock password.** "Forma embaralhada, sem volta" is faithful to "one-way scrambled form" and, like the English, avoids "criptografada". Keep. ("De mão única" would be a closer word for "one-way", but "sem volta" is understood.)
7. **"Nada é enviado" statements.** Literal, and no wider than the English. They are the product claims already checked in `six-traps-preview4.md` (no telemetry, no crash upload, the only outbound calls are the AI providers the user picks with the user's key) and in `local-ai-review.md` (loopback enforced). Nothing new in Portuguese.
8. **"Licença MIT" in the copyright line.** Fine: the licence text stays English (glossary 1.11); only the label is translated.

## Brazilian-law notes (from the statutes as I know them; only the two items marked "web" were checked online)

RapidR Studio is free, local-first, has no accounts, no telemetry, no ads, and sends data only from the user's computer to the AI provider the user picks, with the user's own key; AI is off by default, and the cloud path is worded as 18 or older. That keeps almost everything below out of reach. Conclusion: **no new text is needed in these 120 for Brazilian law beyond the changes above.** Items to keep in mind:

- **LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018).** RapidR collects no personal data of its own, so it has no controller duties (notice under art. 9, data subject rights under art. 18, a data protection officer) for these flows. The provider is the controller of what the user sends. The user (a developer, a school) is the controller of other people's personal data that sits in a project, and art. 4, I excludes only a natural person's private, non-economic use. The texts already say the right things: "dados privados, seus ou de outras pessoas" (S61), "Compartilhe apenas o que você pode compartilhar" (S62), where the data goes and under whose privacy terms (S11, S24-S27), "na China" (S26). Optional, for the Portuguese **manual** ("Privacidade e segurança") rather than for these dialogs (they are long already): one neutral sentence that personal data of other people in a project also goes to the provider, and that leaving Brazil is an international transfer for the person sending it (LGPD art. 33); the cheap way out is a local model. No country is named for Anthropic or OpenAI in the English; naming one is optional and needs a check of each provider's current terms first.
- **Children and adolescents.** ECA (Lei 8.069/1990, art. 2): child up to 12, adolescent 12 to 18. LGPD art. 14: children's and adolescents' data in their best interest; ANPD Enunciado CD/ANPD nº 1/2023 (web): the art. 7 or art. 11 legal bases may be used if the best interest is observed, so parental consent is not the only route. Civil Code arts. 3-4: under 16 cannot contract alone, 16-17 only assisted. This is why providers ask for 18 or a guardian, and why the "18 anos ou mais ... peça a um responsável" sentence (R3) is the right protective text. RapidR itself has no child data to protect; the School Lock, "Off by default" (S40, S74) and Coach mode are the conservative defaults. Keep them.
- **ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025)** (web): in force since 17 March 2026, Decreto 12.880/2026 of 18 March, enforced by ANPD; sanctions reported to start around November 2026 and formal inspections in January 2027 (sources differ on dates). It covers IT products and services directed at minors or "of likely access" by them; the duties include reliable age assurance (self-declaration alone does not count), linking accounts of under-16s to a guardian, parental supervision tools, protective defaults, no profiling of minors for ads, no manipulative design, and a legal representative in Brazil for foreign providers. My reading: Studio is a developer tool with no accounts, ads, profiling, social or chat features, so these duties are not engaged; the "likely access" test is open (and schools use Studio), so this is not certain. Conservative decision, no text change: keep the age wording, keep AI off by default, and **do not add accounts, a cloud service, chat, sharing, ads or telemetry without a new check against this law** (add it to the six-traps routine for Brazil). The age gate for cloud AI is the provider's duty, not RapidR's.
- **CDC (Lei 8.078/1990).** It may not apply to a free tool with no remuneration, but treat it as if it did. Arts. 6, III and 31 want clear, precise, conspicuous information in Portuguese on price and risks; arts. 46 and 54 want terms the consumer can understand, with limiting clauses highlighted; art. 51 voids abusive disclaimers. The 120 texts fit: the price is stated ("Não custa nada" for the local path; the provider's price, in dollars, for the cloud), the risks are stated plainly (AI apps can change and run anything, a real button does it for real, private data is seen), there is no waiver or disclaimer in the dialogs, and nothing is left in English, except the two leaks in R6. Keep it that way: **no limiting or disclaiming sentence in a dialog without highlighting it and a new review.** The pt-BR language is marked "beta, AI translation" in Settings > Language; that is honest and should stay until a native reader has read these 120 texts.
- **Marco legal da IA (PL 2338/2023)**: a bill, not law as far as I could tell; watch it.

Sources checked online: [Machado Meyer on ECA Digital](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/estatuto-digital-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente-lei-n-15-211-2025-entra-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026), [Mayer Brown on ECA Digital enforcement](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies), [Felsberg on ANPD Enunciado 1/2023](https://www.felsberg.com.br/importante-anpd-publica-enunciado-sobre-o-tratamento-de-dados-pessoais-de-criancas-e-adolescentes/). The article numbers of LGPD, CDC, ECA and the Civil Code are from the statutes as I know them and were not re-read.
