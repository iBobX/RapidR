## Law notes (China)

How checked: from knowledge of the statutes, plus these searches on 2026-11-06 (marked "[web]"); everything not marked [web] is from memory and should be confirmed by a PRC lawyer before anyone relies on it.

1. **Is Ruta a "personal information handler" (个人信息处理者, PIPL)?** In the code I read, no: Studio has no accounts, no telemetry and no update check (a search of `ide/*.inc`, `crates/rapidr-studio/src`, `crates/rapidr-ai/src` finds only the providers the user picks, localhost, and GitHub links the user presses); crash reports stay on the computer; "nothing is sent until you send it". So PIPL's notice, consent, local-representative (art. 53) and cross-border-mechanism duties do not attach to Ruta for Studio. PIPL does reach a foreign company that offers products to people in China (art. 3(2)), and a Chinese UI is evidence of that, so the safe state is "collects nothing". **Any** telemetry, update ping, login, cloud sync, RapidR-hosted RAI proxy or shared key, or a website form/analytics aimed at Chinese visitors changes this: new review first (the website checklist already covers the web side).
2. **Cross-border.** The data goes from the user's computer to the provider with the user's own key. The sender is the user. If the project holds other people's personal information (customers, pupils, staff), the user is the handler and PIPL arts. 38-39 (a transfer mechanism, a separate consent) are theirs. The exemptions of the 2024 cross-border provisions (in force 2024-03-22) cover only small volumes of non-sensitive information (under 100,000 individuals a year) and not "important data" [web: [Junhe](https://junhe.com/legal-updates/2401), [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2024-03/22/c_1712776611775634.htm)]; sensitive information (including children's under 14, art. 28/31) has far lower limits. The texts do their part: they name the recipient, say what is sent (message, chat, project parts, window contents), say "in China" for DeepSeek, and say "under its privacy terms". N1/N2 close the two gaps.
3. **Generative AI Interim Measures** (in force 2023-08-15) bind organisations that provide generated text/image/audio/video services to the public in the PRC, "including through an API" (art. 2, 22) [web: [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm), [translation](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/generative-ai-interim/)]. Studio is a **client**: the user picks the provider and makes the key; RapidR hosts no model and no proxy. Keep exactly that structure. Do not, for PRC users, host RAI behind a RapidR server, sell or share keys, or ship a RapidR-branded model as a service without PRC counsel (this includes the planned "RapidR Intelligence" local model: a model run on the user's own computer is different from a service, but the point deserves a look when it exists). Art. 20 lets the regulator act against non-compliant foreign services; that is a reason not to promote Anthropic or OpenAI to PRC readers (N2), not a duty on RapidR.
4. **Minors.** "Under 18" matches the PRC age of a minor, so the five cloud texts and #61 are right as they are. The PIPL "child" line is 14, not 13: that is the handler's duty (GitHub, the AI provider), and RapidR collects nothing. #36 correctly says "GitHub's terms"; keep it as GitHub's rule, not RapidR's. The Regulations on the Protection of Minors Online (2024-01-01) and the 2019 children's-information rules address services that handle minors' data: not triggered while nothing is collected. The Ministry of Education's 2025 guide for pupils' use of generative AI says primary-school pupils should not use open-ended content generation on their own [web: [chinanews](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2025/05-12/10414191.shtml)]; School Lock's Coach and AI-off modes (#69-73) fit that, and a Chinese school will ask. Do not say in Chinese that School Lock "makes a school compliant".
5. **Standard terms** (Civil Code arts. 496-498): see N3. Keep the unambiguous wording of R2, R3.
6. **Absolute words.** In PRC advertising and consumer practice, words such as 绝对, 永远, 100% in claims draw complaints (Advertising Law art. 9 on superlatives, widened in enforcement). In-product help is not advertising, but the same texts get reused in posts and the website. "绝不会" stays: the English "never" is as absolute. "绝对看不到" and "永远" are the changes R1 and C7.
7. **Terms.** The statutes say 个人信息 and 敏感个人信息; the texts say 私人数据 for "private data". Fine for a warning (the English is general), but a Chinese developer may not connect it to their own duties: N1 uses 个人信息 once.
8. **Nothing else found that a free, local-first developer tool needs:** no ICP filing (the web build is served from outside the PRC; a PRC mirror's host files its own), no app-store filing, no Chinese privacy policy for Studio while nothing is collected.

**Where only a native reader or PRC counsel can answer:** (a) whether the 131 texts read as natural, plain Chinese to a teenager and to a developer (the glossary's own §4 list: 令牌/密钥, 钥匙串, 沙盒 vs 沙箱, 服务商, 辅导模式, 学校锁定); (b) whether 绝不会 is acceptable as the rendering of "never" in a product promise to a PRC reader, or whether 不会/从不 is safer (the English is as absolute, so I kept it); (c) any PRC marketing or distribution of the Chinese Studio, and any future collection, hosting or model service; (d) the School Lock texts against a Chinese school's own rules.

# B. The Noto Sans SC subset in the web build

## What was asked

Bundle a subset of Noto Sans SC (SIL OFL 1.1), about 803 KiB, loaded by the web build when a Chinese character is on screen; plus the system's own CJK fonts used at run time without bundling.

## Facts confirmed

- **Licence and holder.** The `LICENSE` in the pinned release archive is the OFL 1.1 in full, with no copyright line and no Reserved Font Name; I fetched the upstream file (notofonts/noto-cjk, `Sans/LICENSE`, main branch) and it opens the same way, with only the definition of "Reserved Font Name" in the text [web: [raw file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/notofonts/noto-cjk/main/Sans/LICENSE)]. The copyright notice is in the font's name table (ID 0, "© 2014-2021 Adobe"), as the lane found, and in the repository's own `fonts/fallback/OFL.txt` ("Noto Sans SC, Noto Sans KR (Noto Sans CJK): (c) 2014-2021 Adobe"). This closes the lane's "not verified: upstream".
- **A subset is a Modified Version.** The OFL FAQ (Q2.6): removing parts of the font, including unused glyphs, is a modification; allowed [web: [OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)]. The 34 chunks already shipped are Modified Versions in the same way.
- **Renaming** is required only when a Reserved Font Name is declared (FAQ Q3.8, Q5.7: no names are reserved by default since 1.1). None is declared, so the OFL does not require a new name. The subset still needs its own family name for a technical reason (the page picks one font per family); see B-2.
- **Bundling with software and selling.** OFL conditions 1 and 2: the font may be bundled, redistributed and sold **with** software; it may not be sold by itself. FAQ Q1.4, Q3.3, Q1.2, Q1.3: software can be sold with the fonts, only the font portions must stay under the OFL, and the OFL fonts can sit beside software under other licences.
- **Policy.** OFL fonts are allowed: memory `dependency-and-portability-policy.md` lists "OFL fonts" among the shipped licences and says "fonts/assets: open licenses only"; `crates/rapidr-cli/src/notices.rs` allows `OFL-1.1`; Liberation, Inter, JetBrains Mono and the Noto chunks already ship under it. The memory's "keep them unmodified" is about Liberation, whose Reserved Font Name forbids modified versions under that name; Noto CJK has none, and modified (chunked, renamed) Noto is already shipped (`LICENSES.md` §4).

The program that loads the font stays MIT (FAQ Q1.2, Q1.3). |
| **B-5** | 4: names of the holders must not promote the modified font | The notice may thank Adobe and the Noto authors; nothing may say they endorse RapidR's subset. | Wording only: "a subset of Noto Sans SC" is an acknowledgement; no "Google's / Adobe's font for RapidR". |
| **B-6** | notices (`tools/third_party_notices.py`, `LICENSES.md` §4, `notices.rs`) | Three places name the Noto fonts today and none knows the subset. | (a) `tools/third_party_notices.py` header text ("Noto Sans SC and KR") add the subset by its new family name; run it, commit the regenerated `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. (b) `LICENSES.md` §4 "Noto fallback fonts": add the subset, its family name, that it is a Modified Version, and the source file's SHA-256 (`faa6c9df…5ea9`) and the pyftsubset options, so it is reproducible. (c) `crates/rapidr-cli/src/notices.rs` (the Noto block, "Shipped beside the web runtime…"): mention the subset, so every web build a user makes (their own Chinese web apps included) carries its own `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` with the OFL text. A test that a web build of a Chinese program has the subset's name in its notices file. |
| **B-7** | GDPR trap 2 (house rule) | The subset is a font fetched by the page. | Same origin only, as the chunks are (`index.json` in the web runtime's own `fonts/`). Never a font CDN or Google Fonts. No new request goes to a third party. |
| **B-8** | provenance | The character list is part of the build. | Generate the list from the GB 2312 table (Python's `gb2312` codec) and the catalogs, not from a downloaded word list; the font file itself comes only from the pinned release (SHA-256 in `fonts.toml`). |

Verdict B: **OK with conditions B-1 to B-8.** None needs a change of plan; B-2 (neutral name, inside the file) is the one that removes the only residual risk (the Noto trademark).

## System fonts at run time: OK

- **Not bundling, not copying.** `chinese_families()` in `crates/rapidr-ui-kernel/src/text/mod.rs` only **names** families (Microsoft YaHei UI / YaHei / SimSun; PingFang SC / Heiti SC; Noto Sans CJK SC and others on Linux) and the OS draws with its own copy. A program that asks the operating system to draw text with a font installed on that computer is the use the font's licence gives with the computer; nothing is copied, embedded or redistributed. A family the computer lacks is skipped. Names used as plain facts (nominative) need no licence.
- **PDF.** `crates/rapidr-value/src/objects/printer.rs` writes the PDF with the standard Helvetica "so nothing is embedded"; no system font reaches a file. **Condition:** if Chinese printing is ever added, embed an OFL font (the Noto subset), never a system font; a font's licence limits embedding in documents.
- **User apps.** The Chinese manual should say once: Microsoft YaHei, SimSun, PingFang and Heiti are licensed for the computers they come with; to ship a Chinese font inside an app or a web page, use an open one such as Noto Sans SC. (Chinese font foundries have sued over unlicensed commercial use of fonts, from memory, e.g. Founder in 2011: this is the likeliest legal contact a Chinese user of RapidR will have with fonts.) Exported apps do not contain system fonts today; keep it so.
- **Screenshots and videos** that show Chinese text drawn by a system font are images of rendered text, not the font software; ordinary practice and a very low risk. If the owner wants zero: take the hero images of the Chinese Studio on Linux with Noto Sans CJK SC (OFL).
- **Linux.** The fonts named there include GPL-with-font-exception and Apache fonts (WenQuanYi, Droid Sans Fallback). Nothing of them is shipped or linked, so no GPL question arises. The "install `fonts-noto-cjk`" note only tells the user; Studio installs nothing (kept so).
- **Windows 11 and macOS captures** used in the repository's docs (`win-after`, `main`) are fine on the same grounds.
